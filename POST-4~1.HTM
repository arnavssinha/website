Materials for 1–3 Pound Combat Robots

~2 min read View original on LinkedIn ↗

Your design only works if you can actually build it. That means knowing what materials work for your weight class and what your Maker Lab can handle.

Aluminum

Aluminum is the default. 6061 is the softer stuff - easy to machine, costs less, good for frames and armor. 7075 is harder and more expensive, better for high-stress parts like motor mounts. The Maker Lab CNC can cut both, no problem.

Steel

Heavy but strong - use it for your weapon. A steel spinner blade hits harder than aluminum; a steel flipper arm absorbs more impact. Do not make your chassis out of steel or you will blow your weight budget. The waterjet cuts steel fast; CNC works too, but slower.

Plastic

HDPE is cheap armor - it absorbs impact well instead of transferring it to your structure. PLA and PLA+ are okay for 3D-printing brackets. ABS is tougher. NylonX is serious nylon reinforced with carbon fiber, way more durable than PLA. The 3D printers in the lab can do all of these.

Titanium and UHMW

Titanium is expensive and overkill for a beginner robot - some advanced teams use it for weapon shafts. UHMW polyethylene is a slippery plastic, good for skids that let your robot slide under opponents without friction eating your battery. CNC can cut both.

The Real Answer

Most competitive 1–3 pound robots use aluminum frames, steel weapons, and plastic armor. That is the balance: strength where it matters, weight savings where possible, and cost that does not bankrupt you. Your choice of material affects everything - what tool you use, how much the part costs, and whether your robot survives impact. Pick wrong and you learn the hard way in the arena.

Introduction to CAD Software - Thinking Before You Cut Expensive Material

~3 min read View original on LinkedIn ↗

IMHO, combat robotics is not about the fighting, it is about the process. It teaches you patience, precision, and pacing. Getting your design right the first time avoids expensive rework and failure in the arena.

Hard-code this into your thinking: combat robots have multiple points of failure and your opponent will find them. The force of impact, the agility of the opponent, their choice of weapon - these will expose every weakness in your design. Think now to avoid a hard lesson later.

Let me illustrate with some real examples. One team added some custom parts and did not recalculate the weight. This was caught at the weigh-in and the last-minute changes significantly reduced its capabilities. In another, the dimensions of the mounting holes were eyeballed, and the final fit could not keep the spinning wheel weapon in place. Another robot had a great design but one hit from the opponent cracked its axle, which had not been properly designed to take the impact of the hit.

That is what CAD is for.

CAD stands for Computer-Aided Design. It is digital sketching and modeling software that lets you think and design parts on a computer before you start building them. Instead of building something and hoping it works, you design it, test it virtually, catch mistakes, and iterate. You will find issues through the process and then you will have to go back to the drawing board. But there is immense value in catching potential problems early.

Why CAD Matters for Combat Robots

CAD lets you think it through without wasting material and, more importantly, your time. When you design in CAD, you can ask real questions: Does this part fit? Is my chassis too heavy? Which material should I use - carbon or titanium? CAD will not catch everything - there will be changes you make through the process - but the initial thinking-through will save you a lot of headaches later.

CAD forces you to think precisely. This precision teaches you how to think like an engineer - you learn that details matter. Additionally, the Maker Lab has equipment that needs digital files. And if you send out for custom manufacturing, the machine shop will only take CAD files.

Why Fusion 360

I recommend Fusion 360 because it is free for students, powerful enough for competition robots, and has become an industry standard. The skills you learn transfer directly to professional engineering if you choose to go in that direction. There are thousands of tutorials and a large community.

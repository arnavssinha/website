Weapon Systems and Drive Systems - The Core of Your Design

~2 min read View original on LinkedIn ↗

Your weapon choice is not just about what hits harder. It changes everything about your robot: weight distribution, battery drain, chassis design, armor placement. You cannot just bolt any weapon onto any frame.

Spinners

A spinning disc or drum weapon. Builds momentum. Hits hard once it is going. The problem: constant battery drain - your motor spins continuously, and if you get flipped you might not have power left to move. Good if you want to win by destroying your opponent. Bad if you get stuck.

Flippers

A pneumatic or servo-powered arm that throws opponents. Requires a compressed air tank or a lot of servo power. Uses less continuous energy than spinners - you can flip, then run away and recharge. But flippers fail if your pneumatics leak or your servo gets weak. More complex to build.

Lifters

Grab and lift your opponent, exposing their wheels. Simple weapon. Uses a lot of torque from your drive motors - you sacrifice some mobility to have strength. Works well in the 1–3 pound class because light robots cannot fight back if inverted.

Pushers

No weapon. Just drive. Wedge under opponents and push them into walls or out of the arena. Simplest design. Cheapest. No extra power drain. Your only job: be faster and more durable than your opponent.

The Cascade Effect

Pick a spinner and you need a beefy motor, a bigger battery, and your weight budget disappears fast. Pick a flipper and you need pneumatic lines, regulators, tanks. Pick a pusher and you have weight left for armor and speed.

Your weapon is your whole strategy. Choose wrong and you spend all season fighting your own design instead of your opponents.

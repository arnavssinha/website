So You Want to Design a Combat Robot?

~2 min read View original on LinkedIn ↗

Hi! I am a junior at Bellarmine College Preparatory in San Jose, and co-president of our Combat Robotics Club. I have spent the last few years designing and building 1–3 pound combat robots, spending multiple hours per week in our Maker Lab with 3D printers, water jets and laser cutters.

I want to make this very clear - Combat Robotics is not really about combat, it is real engineering: CAD design, material selection, manufacturing constraints, testing, and iteration. I learned more about problem-solving and teamwork through robotics than any classroom could teach. It is mechatronics at its finest.

Most people do not have access to school labs like ours. That is why I am publishing this series. I want to show you what is involved and help you get started with whatever resources you have. Ultimately, you will need some place to build the parts - maybe you know a machine shop, or you have the tools in your garage. But I hope I get you started on something that has been an integral part of my life for the last few years. Thank you BCP!

The plan - new posts every two weeks

  • Part 1 - Why Combat Robotics?
  • Part 2 - Introduction to CAD Software
  • Part 3 - Getting Started with Fusion 360
  • Part 4 - Materials for 1–3 Pound Combat Robots
  • Part 5 - Weapon Systems and Drive Systems
  • Part 6 - Designing for Manufacturing
  • Part 7 - Building Resilient Designs
  • Part 8 - From CAD to Manufacturing
  • Part 9 - Assembly, Testing, and Competition
  • Part 10 - Iteration and Improvement

I recommend reading the posts in sequence. You will learn how to design, what materials actually work, how to avoid expensive mistakes, and what is needed to manufacture real parts from digital designs.

Combat robotics teaches you that ideas need to survive contact with reality. You build something, fight it against other robots, and immediately see if your engineering worked. You fail fast and learn faster.

Follow along, and feel free to reach out with questions. Let us build something together.
